After landing in Italy, the second thing to sort out — right after [the tax code](/en/italy-codice-fiscale/) — is mobile data.

Before flying I bought a 14-day pan-European eSIM as a fallback, which I recommend: you will not have a local number the moment you land, and you need connectivity to look things up and receive email while setting the local one up.

## Why iliad

After a bit of research, iliad looked like the friendliest option for a broke student. It sits where a challenger brand usually sits: **cheap and generous, but potentially less rock-solid than the incumbents**.

I went with **GIGA 250**:

![The iliad GIGA 250 offer page](/assets/posts/italy-iliad-sim/iliad-giga250-plan.png "iliad's own GIGA 250 page: 250GB, €11.99 a month *per sempre*, €9.99 activation")

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Monthly | €11.99 |
| Activation | €9.99 (one-off) |
| In Italy | 250 GB (4G / 5G) |
| EU roaming | 18 GB |
| Calls & SMS | Unlimited within Italy |

**Non-EU countries are not covered.** I found this out on a trip to Switzerland — luckily the eSIM I bought back home was still valid.

## Buying it in person

The iliad point we found was tucked inside a Unieuro (an electronics chain). It took us ages to find, and we ended up asking a Decathlon employee for directions. iliad usually operates as a store-within-a-store or a self-service kiosk, so check the exact location on Google Maps before you set out.

We used the iliad Corner inside the Unieuro at Portello. The assistant was friendly, though not much of an English speaker:

What to bring:

- **Passport**
- **Tax code** (knowing the number is enough — no need for the certificate)
- **Your address in Italy**

The process is simpler than expected. Find the staff member at the kiosk and they walk you through it: say which plan you want, choose eSIM or physical SIM, enter your tax code and address, and pick a brand-new number.

Part way through there is an **on-camera identity check** — the assistant asks you to read a passage aloud to the camera. Once that is done you get a confirmation email, and the line works about ten to fifteen minutes after activation.

## After a week of use

**What is good**

- Genuinely fast, and 250 GB is effectively unlimited
- The price is advertised as *per sempre*, so it will not creep up after a few months
- eSIM is issued on the spot, no waiting for a physical card

**What to be ready for**

- **The website and app are Italian-only** — I could not find an English toggle
- **You top up the monthly fee yourself in the app.** I paid by card at the counter without linking it for auto-renewal, so every month is manual
- Coverage is reportedly weaker in the south and in rural areas; if you are not in a large city like Milan or Rome, check the coverage map first

## How iliad compares

The Italian market has two layers: **three network owners** (TIM, Vodafone, WindTre) and the **MVNOs that rent their networks** (ho., Very, Kena and others). iliad is the odd one out — a late entrant that built its own network.

Here are the main options as of September 2026, all with unlimited calls:

| Plan (network) | Monthly | Data in Italy | EU roaming | Activation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **iliad** GIGA 250 (own) | €11.99 | 250 GB | 18 GB | €9.99 |
| **iliad** GIGA 150 (own) | €7.99 | 150 GB | included | €9.99 |
| **ho.** 250 GB (Vodafone) | €8.95 | 250 GB | included | from €2.99 |
| **Very** 300 GB (WindTre) | €7.99 | 300 GB | around 20 GB | free SIM |
| **Kena** (TIM) | €5.99–7.99 | 200–300 GB | 12–15 GB | varies |
| **TIM / Vodafone / WindTre** | from €15 | varies | varies | varies |

A few things the table does not show:

- **Activation price depends on where you come from.** ho.'s €2.99 applies to new numbers or ports from a list of small operators; porting in from TIM, Vodafone, WindTre or Fastweb costs €29.90. Very's cheap tiers are likewise limited to new numbers or ports from iliad, PosteMobile and CoopVoce. A student who just landed and takes a new number normally qualifies for the cheapest tier.
- **Roaming allowances vary a lot.** iliad's GIGA 250 gives a dedicated 18 GB; MVNOs typically land between 12 and 20 GB. If you travel around Europe often, this column matters more than the domestic figure.
- **MVNO promotions move fast.** This table is a September 2026 snapshot and prices may well have changed by the time you read it — check the official site before signing up.

### Which one to pick

- **Travelling around Europe often** → iliad. The most generous roaming allowance, a locked price, and enough retail points to get help in person
- **Only using it in Italy, cheapest wins** → Very or Kena, which give more GB for the same money
- **Best possible coverage, heading to the south or the countryside** → go straight to TIM or Vodafone; more expensive but more reliable
- **English support is non-negotiable** → no Italian carrier is good here, but the big three's shops are more likely to have an English-speaking assistant; with MVNOs you are largely on Google Translate

One last thing: **every carrier needs your tax code first**, so the order is always tax code, then phone plan. [Here is how to get the tax code](/en/italy-codice-fiscale/).
